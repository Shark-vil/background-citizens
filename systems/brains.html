    Actor brains¶

    An actor is split into an actor (data and rules), a body (actor.body: everything that talks to the engine entity: movement, schedules, relationships, weapons, animation) and a brain (actor.brain: decisions). Bodies and brains exist on the server only.

    Pursuing an occluded target¶

    SERVER keeps a stable combat target through GetEnemy: a recent direct attacker takes priority, followed by the siege/squad target and the previous valid enemy. Perception checks every enemy, so a nearer hidden enemy cannot prevent observation of another. Ordinary hidden positions are never refreshed from the current entity position. Teammates, radio backup and siege participants receive the last observed position with its original timestamp; sharing does not renew a sighting.

    When the target is hidden, defense advances to that remembered position without a minimum firing distance. A hidden-target squad uses a column anchored at the remembered position, without the visible-target 550-unit gap. Cover requires line of sight and ends when sight is lost. With no known position, the actor stops rather than navigating to the hidden entity's current position.

    A fixed 1000-unit combat radius (SERVER, no setting) protects a living combat actor from ordinary population removal/recycling and NextBot unstuck teleports while any living known enemy is within that radius, inclusive. It also gates the final respawn operation after asynchronous spawn search. Sieges remain busy until finished. Outside the radius the ordinary unseen timeout and eligibility rules apply. Death, admin cleanup and disabling the addon/type retain their separate paths.

    The 60-second search timeout uses the actor's observation/report timestamp, including its original age when shared. Rewriting an NPC's engine memory to steer pursuit does not renew that timeout. Wanted and always-visible exceptions remain. Body replacement transfers the remembered position, timestamp, stable target and recent attacker. Metrocop equipment uses the same selected target as combat, with the existing arrest-target fallback. Both NextBot unstuck teleport paths check the combat radius; normal movement and optional jumps remain available. See the combat decision tree.

    Reaction to weapons in hands¶

    bgn_react_to_lethal_weapon is a SERVER setting, 0 by default. When enabled, once per second living residents/police actors inspect players, NPCs and NextBots, including BGN actors. A target holding a learned lethal weapon must be within 800 units inclusive, in front of the observer's eyes (FOV, including NextBot observers), and visible through LOS. Shared-team targets, existing enemies, ignored players and players in friend mode are skipped; peaceful mode disables reactions.

    The actor picks GetReactionForDamage(target) (at_damage), as when hurt. ignore/none adds no enemy. Otherwise it requests AddEnemy(target, reaction); if accepted, it stores the reaction and, while calm, clears its targets and enters that state. Busy actors retain their state. Enemy-controller, team, arrest, medic and addon-hook restrictions still apply. This starts the ordinary FSM/GOAP behavior without fabricating damage, conflict records or a wanted assignment. Later police calls and enemy sharing use their ordinary rules; the 800-unit/FOV gate applies to initial detection, not those separate systems. Holstering the weapon or disabling the setting does not clear enemies; ordinary forgetting still applies.

    Learning is always active, including with the checkbox off. On BGN_OnKilledActor, SERVER checks the attacker's callable GetActiveWeapon, validates its returned entity and stores its GetClass(). The last damage must identify that same attacker. Explosion/vehicle damage and kills by a player inside a vehicle are excluded. No prefilled weapon list is supplied; the first qualifying kill teaches a class. Classification uses the weapon held at death, not the projectile/inflictor's class.

    systems/sv_lethal_weapons.lua reads data/background_npcs/lethal_weapons.json once into a persistent SERVER cache, retained through script reloads. The JSON object maps class names to true; all maps share the file. Each new class updates the cache and rewrites the full object; duplicate kills and detection perform no file reads/writes. CLIENT receives only the setting through the existing cvar synchronization. An invalid file is reported and preserved without overwriting for that cache's lifetime; a write failure is reported while the learned class remains in memory. Offline edits take effect on the next server/map start. Isolated tests: tests/sv_lethal_weapons.lua via bgn_selftest.

    Panic and threat search¶

    bgn_threat_search is a SERVER setting, 0 by default (Behavior → AI, Classic → Extensions). When enabled, one tick after BGN_OnKilledActor (after the ordinary witness reactions to the killing damage), SERVER looks for calm living allies of the victim (shared team, not in a vehicle, no enemy in the killer) that see the death and not the killer: within 1500 units of the victim, a clear MASK_BLOCKLOS trace from the victim's position to the actor's eyes (no view cone; the body itself is filtered out), and Combat.CanWitness(actor, killer, true) false. One who sees the killer is left to the ordinary witness reactions. The spawner never recycles or teleports an actor in panic or search_threat. Peaceful mode, ignored killers, non-combatant killers and deaths in a vehicle start nothing.

    • Residents that are neither police nor medics enter panic: a scream, a run to a route point hidden from the killer's position (bgNPC.FindCover now also takes a position), crouching there; after 15-25 s, RandomState.
    • Police and bandits (gangs included) enter search_threat: they go to a random route point within 200 units of where the killer stood, then patrol random points within 600 units of it (a new point on arrival or after 10 s); they walk instead of running within 1000 units of the killer. The search ends after 45 s or when the killer dies.

    The incident keeps the class of the killer's weapon at the moment of the kill (GetActiveWeapon; a weapon in bgn_arrest_safe_weapons, no weapon, or a player in a vehicle count as none). With a class, anyone holding it is a suspect, not only the killer: an innocent with the same weapon can be attacked. Without one, only the killer is recognized in person. Allies, ignored entities and players in friend mode are never suspects. Every 0.33 s (no faster than the brain update: 0.2 s near players, 1 s far away) the actor checks entities within 500 units: distance, view cone, then Vision.HasLineOfSight last. The same suspect in 3 checks in a row is recognized; a miss or another suspect starts over. search_threat then adds it with GetReactionForDamage (ignore/none → defense), panic with run_from_danger. A recognized player alone hears sound/background_npcs/threat_alert.wav from the actor at 60% volume (EmitSound with a one-player RecipientFilter); NPCs and other players hear nothing. A refused AddEnemy keeps the state. An enemy from another system (a shot, enemy sharing) ends either state as an attack does. Isolated tests: tests/sv_threat_search.lua via bgn_selftest.

    Donor animations¶

    bgNPC.Animator plays gestures using a server-owned invisible donor model and clientside bone-merged copies of the character and its weapon. It requires compatible skeletons. Ordinary static sequences still play directly on the actor's body.

    The server checks active animation viewers every 0.25 seconds and sends the current animation when a ready player enters its PVS, including late connections. One-shot animations retain their original deadline; loops last until stopped. Stopping releases an NPC schedule frozen by Animator if it is still current and no static actor animation owns the body. The client copies skin, bodygroups, color, material, submaterials and model scale at animation start; subsequent appearance changes are not tracked. The dance state starts its animation after the previous state's animation is reset.

    SERVER Animator.Play normally requires a PVS viewer at startup. settings.require_pvs = false permits a gameplay animation without one; room sieges use it so a closed visibility portal does not prevent deployment. Late viewers still receive the remaining animation with its original deadline.

    Bodies¶

    bgn_actor_body npc|nextbot (default npc; the old value auto is the same as npc). By default actors are real HL2 NPCs (NPCBody), like in the old versions: other addons (model replacers, ragdoll and death mods, Bio-Annihilation 2, Followers) see only real NPCs (IsNPC, OnNPCKilled). With nextbot, on maps with a NavMesh, actors of the HL2 classes npc_citizen, npc_metropolice and npc_combine_s spawn as bgn_nextbot (NextBot, NextBotBody), and such addons may not work with them. The NextBot walks with the NavMesh path follower, opens doors (the ones allowed, see navigation.md "Doors"), gets unstuck, and shoots or hits with a real HL2 weapon held in its hand (players can't pick it up while the bot is alive; it drops the weapon on death, like an HL2 NPC). Shots start at the bot's right hand. The config weapon_skill works for NextBots too: the spread scales like the HL2 NPC cone (poor ×1.4, average ×1, good ×0.67, very good ×0.33, perfect ×0.2). HL2 NPCs that the bot hates are told to hate it back.

    In nextbot mode, SERVER counts each foreign HL2 NPC's dispositions towards all server players and applies the most frequent disposition (D_HT, D_FR, D_LI, D_NU or D_ER) to nearby BGN NextBot bodies. Ties and an empty player list resolve to D_NU. systems/sv_nextbot_hostility.lua refreshes this once per second within 2500 units, including changes away from hostility. Initialization uses the same vote for NextBot targets. Foreign NPC dispositions towards ordinary HL2 NPC actor bodies are preserved; detection reads their direct disposition towards the actor (see wanted and conflicts). No hidden probe NPCs are created. HL2 NPC senses detect bgn_nextbot through FL_OBJECT, set before engine registration in Initialize. The periodic update is skipped in peaceful mode and with bgn_ignore_another_npc.

    After updating from the probe-based implementation, reload the map to clear legacy probes. bgn_selftest includes the player-disposition vote checks; mixed-player votes are pure logic tests.

    Without a weapon some models (Combine soldiers) have no plain idle/walk/run and would T-pose, so the bot falls back to the armed ones (ACT_IDLE_SMG1, ACT_WALK_RIFLE, ACT_RUN_RIFLE, then the pistol ones). Jumps are off by default (bgn_nextbot_jump, behavior settings): outside the protected combat radius a stuck bot waits and then teleports to the next path point, and a car hit only throws it. With the cvar on, a stuck bot jumps first and a hit adds a jump. An actor leaving a car is put at a spot free for its whole body, never inside the car.

    Weapons and teams¶

    • Only actors whose weapon roll passed (getting_weapon_chance, 10% of citizens) are armed.
    • An unarmed actor in fear / run_from_danger runs to a free weapon within 500 units (pick_up_weapon) and fights with it. It was spawned without a weapon, so it always shoots with WEAPON_PROFICIENCY_POOR (actor.novice_shooter, bgNPC.Combat.ApplyWeaponSkill). The same goes for an HL2 NPC that picked a weapon up by its engine AI.
    • A direct attacker with no shared team is added as an enemy on the first hit, even during an existing fight; reaction hooks can still cancel it.
    • Actors of one team never damage or answer each other. bgn_team_damage 1 lets the damage through, they still don't react to it.
    • Team protection: nothing removes a civilian's residents team, vehicle collisions included (wanted). Ordinary friendly hits/kills do not start fear, retaliation or wanted. bgn_team_damage affects health only.
    • Shared enemies (systems/sv_enemy_sharing.lua, bgn_enemy_share_period, default 2 s, 0 = off): an actor with enemies (not in fear, not in a vehicle) tells every teammate within 1000 units that sees it (a trace from the teammate's eyes to the actor) about the enemies the teammate does not know. A teammate in a car is skipped, except an officer: it gets the enemy and steps out or fires from the car (modules/dv/sv_move_to_target.lua). A teammate in a calm state switches to its GetReactionForProtect reaction; a busy one only gets the enemy. This keeps the others from walking around while an officer already shoots at a player who is not wanted yet. Gang exceptions (all visible allies, without a distance cap or reaction roll) are described under gangs.
    • A wanted actor (Actor:IsWanted, wanted is lost by NPCs exactly like by players) hides: a defense reaction turns into retreat, which fights back only when the enemy is within 190 units; defense returns to retreat when the enemy is over 300 units away and the actor has under 50% of its max health. Witness check (same timer): a calm actor not in a vehicle that sees a teammate in fear, run_from_danger or dyspnea_danger with enemies, and itself sees one of that teammate's enemies within 1000 units, takes the enemy too and reacts like a calm one that was asked for help. Fights and other danger states do not count. The scan starts from scared actors within 2500 units of a living player, so no traces are spent far from players. It needs both lines of sight, so a scared actor's panic spreads only as far as eyes reach. AddEnemy guards (arrest protection, ignored entities, peaceful mode) still apply.

    Prop-chair seating (sit_to_chair) uses the configured chair models and offsets. The route handles the distant approach; within 300 units the body finishes moving to the chair, and seating begins within 60 units. This applies to both HL2 NPC and NextBot bodies. Custom map seats use sit_to_chair_2.

    Choosing a brain¶

    bgn_brain auto|fsm|goap (default fsm, the classic brain). In auto an actor gets the brain from its config field brain = 'fsm' | 'goap', otherwise GOAP when the config has weapons, otherwise FSM.

    FSM brain¶

    Runs the update callback of the current state (bgNPC.States), and picks a random state from at_random when the actor has none. Reactions to damage and threats are random picks from at_damage / at_protect.

    GOAP brain¶

    Plans only the fight; everything else is the FSM brain. The config reactions (at_damage / at_protect, a weighted random pick, the same as in FSM) decide whether the actor fights at all: an officer that rolled arrest arrests, a citizen that rolled fear runs. The plan takes over once the actor is in defense (or retreat, the defense of a wanted actor) and keeps it until the enemies are gone or the state leaves the plan's own states (the FSM ended the fight, an arrest, a calm state); it never pulls the actor back into a fight. Having an enemy alone (shared enemies, a wanted target) does not start one. Earlier 2.3.1 builds also picked the reactions from the plan and attacked whenever the actor had an enemy, which made fights much more frequent than with FSM.

    • Facts are measured by sensors once per second (and right after damage or a new enemy): has_enemy, armed, is_police, low_health, enemy_close, enemy_armed, enemy_police, police_called, wanted, in_cover, backup_called, outnumbered, surrendered, desperate, can_hold_door, enemy_bracing, can_siege. outnumbered is police only: two or more living enemies of the officer within 2000 units, and more of them than living officers there (itself included).
    • Actions run states: attack → defense, flee → fear, call_police → calling_police, hide → retreat (wanted actors only), take_cover → take_cover, call_backup → call_backup, surrender → arrest_surrender, hold_door → hold_door, breach_door → breach_door, siege → siege. An actor can only use actions whose state appears in its at_damage / at_protect config with a weight above zero, except actions registered with always = true (hide, take_cover, call_backup, surrender, hold_door, breach_door, siege). A trait action is rolled once per actor: call_police with its config share (20 of 100 → 20%). surrender is not a trait: it is rolled at every attempt (8%, see below). An action stays running while its state moves on to a follow-up (also, e.g. fear → run_from_danger). Any state change re-reads the facts at once, so an action that finished with its effects (take_cover sets in_cover, call_backup sets backup_called) moves the plan on instead of failing. An action whose state is already running (also after a replan) counts as entered. An action whose state moves to another action's state without its effects is blocked for 5 s, then the plan is made again; a state outside the plan ends the fight.
    • Reactions (GetReactionForDamage / GetReactionForProtect) are the config roll, as in FSM. In fear an armed actor switches to defense (as in FSM) unless the plan itself sent it to flee.
    • Chains. Fighting an armed enemy or, for an outnumbered officer, a crowd costs more out of cover, and an outnumbered officer without backup pays more again, so the plans are take_cover → attack and, for an outnumbered officer, take_cover → call_backup → attack (no cover found: call_backup → attack in the open):
    • take_cover: runs to the nearest nav point within 1000 units the enemy can't see at crouch height (bgNPC.FindCover), crouches 3 s, then fights 10 s (in_cover) before the next cover. No cover found: it fights.
    • call_backup: the outnumbered officer calls on the radio (police_backup); up to 3 free officers on foot within 4000 units take the enemy and come, and with bgn_enable_wanted_mode on every enemy of the officer (except police) becomes wanted, which sends all the police after it. Once per officer per minute. Earlier 2.3.1 builds called backup against any armed enemy, without the wanted status.
    • surrender: a hurt gangster (low_health) facing the police gives up (goal give_up, above hiding) when an officer within 700 units sees it and bgn_arrest_mode is on, and only with an 8% roll per attempt (a failed roll blocks the action for 5 s, so the roll repeats every few seconds while the conditions hold): it says give_up, the officer (stepping out of its car if needed) arrests it through ArrestModule:Surrender (systems/arrest). The plan never interrupts arrest_surrender.
    • Arrests are not planned: the arrest system (systems/arrest, ArrestModule:CanArrest) owns them, and it only works on police without enemies.
    • Goals (eliminate, survive, report, hide, give_up, shelter) get a priority from the actor's set: police, gangster or citizen (by team, or the config field goap_set). hide outranks ordinary combat while wanted; an available door shelter has priority 30 when desperate. A citizen reports (calls the police) an enemy farther than 700 units even when it is armed, unless it is hurt (then it runs) or armed itself (then it fights).
    • The planner (A* over fact bit masks) returns the cheapest action chain for the top goal. Plans are cached.
    • ignore in a reaction table keeps its random chance.

    Debug: bgn_goap_debug prints the set, goal, plan and facts of the actor you look at.

    Door shelter and breaching¶

    These are server-driven actions for both NPC and NextBot bodies, not random scenic poses. Any non-police GOAP actor already fighting or fleeing can seek an open door when below 35% health, unarmed against an armed enemy, or facing more living enemies within 1000 units than itself plus nearby armed teammates. An ordinary flee reaction only borrows GOAP to seek shelter; it does not become an attack if no door fits or shelter ends.

    hold_door searches within 400 units for an unlocked, unclaimed rotating door. It uses the leaf's closed geometry, moves to the side away from the enemy, stays outside the swing while closing, temporarily locks the door, then steps up and plays a supported doorbracer_* sequence. Approaching takes at most 10 s, closing/positioning 4 s, holding 12 s; another attempt waits 20 s. The action ends if the enemy dies, reaches the same side, disappears from the actor's enemies, or the door is removed. It releases its lock on exit. Models are never replaced to obtain an animation: without a supported brace sequence the action is unavailable. Supported doors are upright, unparented prop_door_rotating and func_door_rotating; sliding and moving-parent doors are skipped. Physics/world obstructions may prevent reaching or closing a door, ending the attempt.

    Police with an enemy actively holding the other side can choose breach_door within 800 units. They approach, kick (when the model has a suitable sequence), and open the door at 900 degrees/s, restoring its previous speed after 0.4 s. This only releases the holding actor's temporary lock; it does not force arbitrary map-locked doors.

    The holder enters knocked_down: a temporary server ragdoll receives the impulse, while the same original NPC/NextBot entity and actor/UID remain registered, hidden, non-solid and unable to move, shoot or board a car. The breach and physics contacts add no damage. Shots at the ragdoll are forwarded to the actor; lethal damage uses ordinary death/corpse handling. After 3 s it looks for a full standing hull within 96 units of the body; if blocked, it stays down and retries. On recovery it restores visibility, collision, equipment and the previous combat/flee behavior, keeping current health. Removing the temporary ragdoll also removes its hidden actor. Clients only display the server state/body; they do not choose actions or control doors.

    Sieging a single-exit room¶

    SERVER GOAP fighters of class npc_metropolice or npc_combine_s can choose siege for an enemy last seen within 30 s, at a doorway within 1000 units. Enemy memory stores the last visible position; the tactic does not aim at a continuously updated position through walls. It requires a loaded NavMesh, including when pedestrian routes use the custom provider. Removing the doorway connections must leave a roofed pocket containing the remembered position, with no alternate connection around the cut. Adjacent door leaves count as one exit; narrow walled NavMesh passages without door entities are also considered if door candidates do not prove a pocket. If the target vanished within 300 units of a doorway and the remembered side proves no pocket, the far side of that doorway is tried as the pocket. An actor already inside the pocket never joins; a member that enters it leaves the siege for defense without cancelling the others. Searches are bounded to 128 areas, 900 units and one floor (80-unit height tolerance); ladders, unknown geometry, missing navigation or an unproven pocket decline the tactic. The scenic indoor check alone does not prove one exit. Incomplete NavMeshes can omit real escapes such as windows or jumps.

    One reservation controls the enemy/entrance. Up to five members of the initiating squad occupy separated positions outside it; other armed fighting teammates within 2000 units receive spaced waiting positions at least 650 units away (34-unit arrival tolerance). Existing squad membership is captured before the normal squad rebuild excludes siege participants. Without a current squad, the nearest eligible allies fill the five places. Only actors with a supported waiting animation join. Positioning has a 15 s limit; the 12 s silent wait starts after the front group is positioned and rear participants have withdrawn. Both NPC and NextBot bodies suppress ordinary shooting during the tactic; siege radio lines remain enabled. Followers can execute the state with an FSM brain; the initiating fighter requires GOAP.

    An officer holding fire for negotiation cannot initiate or join a siege. Negotiation also excludes actors already in a siege, so the two systems cannot restore each other's weapon capabilities.

    Narrow corridors fall back to separated positions along the passage instead of side positions. Members that cannot fit near the entrance try the rear positions; if a close eligible reinforcement cannot withdraw, the attempt ends rather than leaving an uncoordinated group crowding the active siege.

    After waiting, closed rotating doors are kicked with 35% probability or opened normally. An unsupported kick falls back to a supported opening gesture, and vice versa; without either animation the doorway is unavailable. Combine models can use their gun strike to breach. Existing door-opening/locked-door settings and held-door reservations still apply. The operator steps outside the swing and retries opening once if necessary. Doors must have started opening before deployment, and the payload path must be clear. Their automatic return delay is temporarily suspended and restored on exit; a previously locked door is relocked after the attempt. Door entities are preserved.

    The single operator deploys one npc_manhack (npc_metropolice, requires deploy) or one npc_grenade_frag (npc_combine_s, requires grenThrow). The selected model is never replaced. Existing donor animation playback prevents native NPC animation events from creating duplicate weapons. The grenade uses a traced ballistic path, a 3.5 s fuse and a blast-clearance check for non-enemy actors/players. The manhack gets a checked entry direction and two seconds of native directed flight, then ordinary AI; it is hostile (D_HT) to every current enemy of the operator, friendly (D_LI) to the operator's team, neutral (D_NU) to everyone else, never becomes wanted, is skipped by the relationship and NextBot-hostility updaters that would override its relations, and removes itself after 30 s. Weapon damage is attributed to its operator. The operator opens the door and releases the weapon from the same spot: the throw starts while the leaf is still swinging (a retry window of 1.5 s covers a blocked path), and the operator then returns to its slot during the fuse. The operator is not part of the blast-clearance check. The group holds another 6 s after deployment. The whole attempt is limited to 50 s and shares a 60 s target cooldown. Damage to a participant, loss of an enemy/operator, leaving the pocket, interruption by arrest or another state, map cleanup or a navigation reload ends it and restores body control. Clients display the state/animations; they do not choose the tactic or spawn weapons.

    Hostage negotiation¶

    systems/sv_negotiation.lua (SERVER). A frightened non-police actor within 100 units of a player target that stands closer to the officer than the target is a hostage: the target hides behind it, so approaching from behind does not count. Every officer whose selected target meets this rule holds fire (engine NPCs lose CAP_USE_WEAPONS, NextBots skip UpdateCombat), the first one offers to surrender and warns 5 s before the 20 s deadline. A negotiating officer in defense keeps its distance: closer than 600 units it stops approaching (squad slots included), closer than 400 it backs off. The talks end quietly when the shield stays gone for 3 s; they fail on the deadline or when the player kills anyone. After a failure the officers shoot again and the player is not offered talks until he is arrested, drops a wanted level, dies or 120 s pass. Negotiation with NPC targets is a separate story scenario and is not implemented.

    The hostage test currently uses radius, fear state and relative distance only; it does not check LOS to the hostage or alignment with the firing line. This rule has not been changed by the combat audit fixes.

    Extending¶

    bgNPC.GOAP.RegisterAction('duck', {
    state = 'retreat',                      -- a registered state that performs the action
    pre = { has_enemy = true, enemy_armed = true },
    effects = { safe = true },
    cost = function(ws) return 1 end,       -- number or function(world_state)
    -- optional: always = true (no config weight needed), trait = true + chance = 0.3 (rolled once per actor),
    -- prepare = function(actor) return true end (sets up before the state starts; false blocks the action)
})
bgNPC.GOAP.sensors.my_fact = function(actor, npc, enemy) return ... end
bgNPC.GOAP.RegisterSet('my_set', { survive = function(ws) return 10 end })

    Squads¶

    bgn_module_tactical_groups (on by default). Every second actors in defense of one team within 1200 units form squads of up to 5. The healthiest leads, its selected combat enemy becomes the target of every member. Formations face the target: column when nobody sees it, wedge with 4+ healthy members, line otherwise. The healthiest stand in front, the wounded (< 40% health) form a rear rank. defense moves a member to actor.squad.slot; bgNPC.Squads.list holds the current squads.

    LOD¶

    Actors far from players update less often (bgn_lod_near_radius); idle unarmed actors out of range sleep (bgn_disable_logic_radius). Hook: BGN_ActorLODChanged(actor, lod) (1 near, 2 far, 3 dormant).

    Distant unseen unarmed actors on foot now alternate pauses and active windows instead of remaining frozen. SERVER settings (Performance and classic Optimization): bgn_logic_pause = 2 s (0 disables pauses), bgn_logic_active = 1 s (minimum 0.1), bgn_logic_stagger = 0.25 s per UID. During active windows both engine movement and the brain resume; brain interval is 0.2 s. Phase is (time - UID * stagger) modulo (pause + active), stable across list reordering. Windows overlap and phases wrap in larger crowds; this spreads transitions, not exclusive execution. Close/in-view actors, armed actors and vehicle occupants retain continuous simulation. Damage/enemy events immediately wake an actor for at least one second. LOD 3 now means intermittent simulation, not permanent sleep. Existing distance thresholds are retained; with near radius 2500 and disable radius 500, off-view actors beyond 2500 enter this cycle. Timings are observed on actor scheduler passes, not exact real-time deadlines.

    Medics¶

    Actor type medic (config/actors/sh_medic.lua, limit 4: two pairs) is never spawned by the regular spawner. Every actor death outside a vehicle is a call (bgNPC.Medics.calls, kept 90 s). A free medic takes it, otherwise one is spawned near the body, with an ambulance when Decent Vehicle is on and the config has vehicles. The ambulance drives to the body like a police car on a chase, the medic gets out, kneels over the body for 6 s with voice lines and medkit sounds (state medic_revive), the actor comes back (same type, model and skin; its ragdoll is removed) and the medic returns to the car. A call closes when the body is gone (a client reports its ragdoll faded or was cleaned up, or the server ragdoll was removed) or after 90 s; the medic then walks back to its car and drives on with the siren off. The last 300 units to the body the medic walks straight, off the nav points. Some deaths cannot be saved: explosions, vehicle hits and overkill damage always, any other death with the chance bgn_medic_hopeless_chance (25 %; Behavior > Medics). For those the medic still comes and kneels over the body, but instead of reviving says medic_cannot_save and leaves (call.verdict); only then does an officer take the body. Medics only take as enemies those who hurt them. Nobody comes for an actor killed by the police (team police), nor for one whose death no living ally of the victim saw, nor for an enemy of the residents or one whose team is fighting them.

    When a medic is killed, medic spawns and responses are blocked around the death position. Existing calls in that area pause: their responders stop heading to the body and return to their ambulance when possible. bgn_medic_danger_time defaults to 60 seconds (0 disables the restriction), and bgn_medic_danger_radius defaults to 1500 units; both are in the behavior settings under Medics. The area reopens when the timer expires or the killer dies (player, NPC or NextBot actor). Areas belonging to other killers remain blocked. Pending calls can resume while their original 90-second lifetime remains; the restriction does not extend that lifetime. Hook: BGN_ActorRevived(actor, call).

    • Pairs: a medic spawned for a call brings a partner when the limit allows (medic_partner on both). It rides in the same ambulance (seats: 2), gets out with the medic, kneels beside the body (call.partner) and gets back in with it; a partner within 400 units of the car is pulled in when the medic takes the wheel.
    • Ambulances: no new one when a player near the call (3000 units) already has an ambulance around; an empty ambulance there is taken instead.
    • Attacked: by a player, the medic reviving keeps reviving (a life first) and its partner fights with fists (state brawl); a medic not reviving fights itself. Below 30 % health a medic leaves the call (the partner takes it over) and runs, by its car when one is near (go_to_police_car, then fear). Attacked by an actor, it runs as before.

    Gangs¶

    Two street gangs replace the plain gangster actor (kept disabled as their config base): Black Raven (gangster_crows, team gang_crows) and Red Wolf (gangster_wolves, team gang_wolves) (config/actors/sh_gangs.lua). Both keep the team bandits (police reactions, carjacking). A member is light (35 % are heavy, heavy_chance): light members wear HL2 citizen clothes and carry a pistol, heavy ones wear rebel gear and carry an SMG or a rifle (systems/npcs/sv_gang_models.lua). The clothes sheet of the model is replaced by the gang's recolor with SetSubMaterial (materials/background_npcs/gangs/<gang>/<class>_<sex>); the class is read from the model, so it survives revival and leaving a car. The recolors are made from the game's own textures by tools/make_gang_skins.py (needs vpk, srctools, pillow, numpy); the game files are not shipped.

    Gang support and walking. Members walk in packs of up to three; all visible same-gang allies receive a threat without a chance roll or roster cap, including late arrivals. Combat support is independent of walking packs. See gangs for visibility, sharing interval and war rules.

    Orphan bodies: Actor:RemoveActor leaves the entity to the engine, right for a death only. Every 2 s an NPC or NextBot that is an actor body (BGN_ACTOR_OBJECT, or bgn_npc_class from the spawner) without a live registered actor is removed after 3 seconds, even with bgn_enable 0 (spawner/sv_actor_remover.lua, with developer 1 the console names its class, health, type and last state). Health is not checked: an NPC killed while the LOD had its think turned off, or frozen by the animator, never finishes dying and stands with 0 health, frozen, without a label; the old check (alive bodies only) left those on the map for good. An actor removed in its first tick is no longer registered afterwards (it kept a slot of the type limit). A medic marked eternal for a call is released together with the call, so it no longer escapes the limit and the remover.

    Calling the police is skipped when the target is already wanted, or at least one living officer has it as an enemy or a target (bgNPC.PoliceKnows, calling_police state and the GOAP police_called sensor).

    Delay between skirmishes¶

    bgn_fight_cooldown (Behavior > AI, 0-120 s, default 60, 0 = off, the old behavior). With a delay, the first defense reaction of the at_protect table (actors protecting their own) opens a window of that many seconds around the place (2500 units). While it lasts, protecting against another attacker in that area is ignored: the actor takes the ignore reaction and a call for help, enemy sharing or a witnessed hit adds no enemy to it. The police are never delayed, also an officer driving past in a car. Actors joining the skirmish that opened the window (same attacker), fights already going, places outside the area and the time after the window are not affected. A player, or an NPC / NextBot of another mod that is not one of our actors, as the attacker is never blocked (systems/sv_fight_cooldown.lua).

    Police at a body¶

    Every actor death outside a vehicle also leaves a body for 60 s (brain/fsm/states/sv_police_inspect_body.lua). The nearest officer within 2000 units who walks or idles without enemies takes it (state police_inspect_body): runs to the body when it is farther than 600 units (walks when closer), says police_body_arrive, crouches over it, reports it on the radio (police_body_report) and writes it down (police_body_notes), 5 s each, then goes on patrolling. Bodies from a shoot-out are taken once the officers calm down; an enemy that appears during the inspection ends it at once and the officer reacts as to an attack. Bodies within 150 units are one scene with one officer. A body revived by a medic is dropped. A body a medic is on the way to waits until the medic has revived it or declared it hopeless.

    Onlookers¶

    Every actor death outside a vehicle is also an incident for 45 s (brain/fsm/states/sv_watch_incident.lua). From 5 s after it, calm civilians walking or idling within 1500 units come one by one (35% per 2 s check, up to 4): they stand 150-280 units from the spot on their side, stare (LineIdle*), half of them say a watch_incident line, and go on after 12-25 s. A gunshot scares them like any calm actor; two of them may start a conversation. When the medics revive the actor (BGN_ActorRevived), the incident ends: no new onlookers come and those already there go on.

    Bodies¶

    systems/sv_corpses.lua, systems/cl_corpses.lua. Settings window: Behavior > Bodies.

    Cvar (server) Default Meaning
    bgn_corpse_cleanup 0 the bodies of dead actors (NPC and NextBot) fade out (off by default)
    bgn_corpse_cleanup_time 60 seconds after the death

    A body a medic is assigned to, or an officer is inspecting, waits until they are done (both end on their own: the call after 90 s, the inspection after its give-up time). Server ragdolls fade on the server, client ragdolls on every client. npc_battle_cleanup would remove a body in seconds, before a medic or an officer gets to it, so the actors' bodies are left to this setting: server ragdolls get its doNotRemove flag, its client-side corpse cleanup skips them. With bgn_corpse_cleanup 0 (the default) the flag is not set and npc_battle_cleanup, if installed, removes the bodies as usual; without it they stay until the engine's own ragdoll limits remove them.

    Cars¶

    Police dismount when a living enemy (or their current arrest target) is within 800 units or their vehicle has at most 20% health remaining. They stay seated while that enemy is driving away: the dismount waits until the enemy's car has stood still for about 6 seconds or the enemy gets out.

    A police car chasing a wanted player that falls more than 2800 units behind and is out of every player's view is not culled: its crew steps out and the car is rebuilt (a fresh car, the same actors and enemies) on a hidden road point 600-2200 units from the player, at most once per 8 seconds per player and only while fewer than 1 + wanted level chasing cars are close. See spawner/sv_police_pursuit.lua. Spawn points also keep clear of the road ahead of a moving player. Simfphys health uses GetCurHealth() and GetMaxHealth(); vehicles without a positive maximum health do not trigger the damage condition. Neutral players do not trigger dismounts. Hits use the normal actor damage reactions, so a real attack can create an enemy and make police dismount. Direct vehicle removal removes its crew; forced ejection still requires a safe exit.

    Actors and Decent Vehicle cars; the state go_to_police_car (no longer police only) runs to the car and gets in.

    • Back to the car (modules/dv/sv_return_to_vehicle.lua, every 2 s): an actor within 1000 units of an allowed car returns when no enemy is within 1500 units. Neutral living players also delay civilians, but not police. Former crew may take free passenger seats after their driver returns. Police do not reboard critically damaged cars.
    • The enemy drives off (sv_defense.lua, every 2 s, 30 %): a player in a vehicle or an actor in a car; checked before the squad move, so squad members go too.
    • Confiscation and carjacking (data.seize): when no car of its own is near, the police take a car of another group ("police_seize") and bandits carjack one ("gangster_carjack"): whoever sits inside is thrown out (VEHICLE:Eject, scared unless it is a teammate) and the car joins the actor's group (VEHICLE:SetGroup: the police get the chase AI). Bandits on the run (retreat, fear, run_from_danger) within 700 units of such a car take it too (35 % per check) and keep running in it.

    NextBots and cars (systems/sv_nextbot_vehicle_hits.lua): a NextBot has no physics object, so the engine deals it no crush damage. Every 0.1 s each moving car (players', Decent Vehicle ones) faster than 150 u/s hurts NextBots in its box and just ahead ((speed - 75) x 0.25, the driver is the attacker) and throws them. A NextBot whose enemy sits in a car shoots the car (the enemy is the car then, which has no health of its own).

    Which car: VEHICLE:CanBoard: an empty one the actor left, or of its group (police, bandits, medics, ...), never someone else's residents car (police and medics are residents too, citizens keep to their own). Former crew may also rejoin an occupied car if a passenger seat is free. A police vehicle keeps its police designation even after a group change; only police actors may drive or ride in it. Players can still take an empty police car. VEHICLE:CanSeize: a car of another group, empty or driven by an actor (never a player), not driven by a teammate. Only the police and bandits seize.

    Boarding removes the physical NPC/NextBot and binds the same logical actor to a DV anim entity; exiting recreates its original body and equipment. Exit placement prefers the closest model exit or the side and row of its own seat, checking full body clearance and overlap even with non-colliding actors. If all positions are blocked, it stays aboard. Seated hits retain the attacker and notify nearby witnesses through the normal damage hooks. Witness traces exclude their own chassis/seat and recognize another occupant's chassis; walls still block sight.

    BGN traffic can plan bounded forward/reverse maneuvers around blockers and, after repeated failures, try another accessible road segment. Red lights, scheduled stops and taxi waits suppress recovery. Cars honk briefly only at an entity blocking their forward sensor. These changes apply to BGN-created vehicles; DV remains required. See traffic checks and limitations.

    Related systems¶

    Gangs walk in packs of three and support visible allies; direct self-defense remains. Medics and bodies own calls/verdicts/cleanup. Wanted and arrest own pursuit/surrender. Stuck NextBots choose nearby free navigation space excluding vehicles; custom-chair placement uses seat height.

    Police weapon choice and witnessed harm¶

    See police weapon choice and harm memory.

    NextBot pistol cadence¶

    The stock pistol now fires groups of 1–3 individual shots, approximately 0.2 s apart, followed by a 0.45–0.8 s rest. These are separate, visibility/friendly-fire-checked shots, not multiple bullets in one attack. The 18-round magazine keeps the 1.8 s reload pause. Burst state resets on weapon/target changes and reload; other weapon profiles and scripted-weapon fallback remain unchanged. Combat checks quantize the intended intervals to engine ticks.

    python tools/test_police_force.py checks witnessed distinct counts, thresholds, memory/reset/rebind, vehicle damage and pistol cadence. Live single-player checks compare native HL2 and NextBot shots and inspect arrest weapon retention; multiplayer and third-party weapon bases require separate verification.

    Movement updates run every 0.1 s for near actors and every 0.25 s for far or dormant ones (the node arrival radius is 150 units).
